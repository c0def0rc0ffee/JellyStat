How these are chosen

Entirely from your own data, and from three separate things: the community rating, how you rate that sort of thing compared with your own average, and how much of your viewing is in its genres. The middle one is why this differs from a most-watched list — a genre you watch constantly is not thereby a genre you enjoy, and your own scores are the only record of the difference. Genres you have rated little are trusted less, so the picture sharpens as you rate more. Nothing leaves the box and no external service is consulted; what counts as eligible follows the Recommendations setting.

Jellyfin agreement

JellyStat holds what you think of a title; Jellyfin keeps its own copy. Jellyfin missing a rating is harmless. Jellyfin holding a different one is a contradiction nobody can settle from the data, so those are listed here and fixed deliberately: either push JellyStat's score across, or remove Jellyfin's so only one copy exists.

Import from a Trakt export file

Ask Trakt for an export of your data, then select the whole folder here. The files are read in the browser and matched against your library: you see what was found, choose how it should meet what you already have, and nothing is written until you do. A backup is taken either side.

Automatic backups

Taken either side of every import, newest first. The most recent 12 are kept.

Import watch history

Backfill the play log from a file: a Jellyfin Playback Reporting backup, another box's JellyStat history.db, a Trakt or Letterboxd export, or a generic CSV/JSON with started_at, media, title columns. Nothing is written until you review it below and choose how to apply it.
